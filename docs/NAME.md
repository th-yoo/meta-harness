# kkamak (까막)

**Project name, sealed 2026-07-25.** Pronounced *"kkah-mahk"*. Korean for the
crow (까마귀 / 까막).

## Derivation — Aesop's crow, in Korean

The name IS the Aesop fable bird — the crow/jackdaw in borrowed plumes —
named in the owner's language. The naming question began with an originality
audit (2026-07-25, resume.md ledger): most of this project's components are
borrowed by design (AHE's method, Stop-hook and MutGen patterns, curated
prompts, classical statistics). The fable fits with one inversion that IS our
originality: **this crow runs the plucking itself.** The adoption gate,
guards, and held-out arms are the flock reclaiming feathers on schedule —
v8, v9, v10 = plumes stripped, publicly, in the ledger. What the bird still
wears, it wears because the flock already attacked it and it held.

(NOT the derivation, recorded to prevent later confusion: 오작교/까막까치 and
the "crow flies, pear falls" proverb were considered as rationales during
naming and are pleasant resonances at most — the name derives from the Aesop
crow, nothing else.)

## Candidates considered

- **Jackdaw** — rejected on collisions: two established Kafka libraries
  (FundingCircle, obsidiandynamics), a known AD-recon infosec tool (PyPI),
  Shell's Jackdaw gas field dominating search, and an AI-sessions product
  (jackdaw.whattheflock.dev) squatting our exact category.
- **Graculus** (Latin jackdaw, Phaedrus' *Graculus superbus et pavo*) —
  collision-clean (only a Yorkshire copper-bird sculptor and a dormant UK
  ltd) but a fetched Latin feather.
- **kkamak** — chosen: the same fable bird in the owner's own language;
  collision-clean in software (only a Korean game streamer's handle and
  unrelated differently-spelled companies); short; CLI-fine.

A coincidence recorded for the record: the naming session ran on a model
named Fable — the fable model, mid-fable, naming the fable's crow.
